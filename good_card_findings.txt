Key Findings
1. Positive symbol COUNT matters most. Not net value, not arrows — just having many positive symbols. Card 1 and Card 10 both have 8 positives and both rank top 3.
2. Symbol diversity pays because of the scoring curves. Each symbol type has diminishing returns (e.g., 5 coins = 7 pts but 3+2 of something else scores more). Card 1 has 6 different positive types (Par, Coi, Rum, Spy, Map, Anc) — maximum diversity.
3. Parrots are the best symbol (r=+0.585). Parrots score 3 pts for just 2, and scale to 15 pts for 12. Cards with parrots consistently rank higher.
4. Rats are the worst symbol (r=−0.516). Rats are more damaging than sharks or krakens because they accumulate: each additional rat adds increasing penalty (−1, −1, −2, −2, −3...).
5. Krakens show positive correlation (+0.201) which seems paradoxical. This is because krakens have a capped penalty (−4 for 1, flattening to 0 for 5+). A card with 1 kraken alongside many positives (like Card 14: Kra×2 + 6 positives) still performs well — the kraken damage is bounded.
6. Hide gain is negatively correlated — cards that NEED quarter hiding (concentrated negatives) still perform worse than cards that don't need it at all. Having 3 negatives in one quarter is better than having them spread, but having 0 negatives is better still.
7. Arrows are nearly uncorrelated with card value (r=−0.076). This is surprising — arrows add variance (as we saw earlier) but don't reliably add VALUE. Their worth depends entirely on what neighbors they copy, which is random. Card 18 has 2 arrows but ranks #19 because its base symbols are weak.
8. Overperformers (better than stats predict): Cards 14 and 0 both overperform their raw stats. Card 14 has 4 negatives but still ranks #2 — its positive symbols (Rum, Anc, Par, Map, Coi) are high-diversity and high-value, and the negatives are in coverable positions. Card 0 has net −1 value but ranks #8 — hiding its TR quarter (Shk+Rat) turns it positive.
